#### Episode 1 — July–September 2025 (the major episode)

**Course.** Limp noticed ~25.07.2025 after roughly two hours unobserved at home `[O]`. Over three
weeks it progressed from grade 2/4 to complete non-weight-bearing (5/5), unresponsive to two
NSAIDs, then to a swollen, painful limb up to the elbow with regional lymphadenopathy.

**The diagnostic detour.** On 15.08.2025 a **fracture of digit IV was suspected on radiographs
read by a veterinary assistant (PTA)** and a cast was applied under anaesthesia `[S9]`. On
21.08.2025 Dr. Wendler examined Gatsby and explicitly **did not accept this**: he found the
shoulder and elbow non-painful, the **carpus clearly painful on flexion**, and only questionable
pain at digit IV — noting that the toe finding *"does not on its own explain the massive
lameness."* He recorded the radiographic appearance as a *questionable porous bone structure* of
digit IV, left the diagnosis **open**, and referred to the Tierspital for CT `[S9]`.
> **The fracture was never confirmed and is not part of the final diagnosis.** Any earlier note
> describing a "chip fracture of the middle phalanx" is superseded — see §13.

**Tierspital findings, 25–27.08.2025 `[S2]`**

*Clinical:* vital parameters normal; lameness 5/5; swelling of the paw extending to the proximal
antebrachium; pain on lateral palpation of the entire limb; enlarged left prescapular lymph node;
**a small wound with redness in the web between digits 3 and 4.**

*CT with iodinated contrast:*
- Poorly defined, permeative lysis at the **distal Os metacarpale IV**, more pronounced abaxially
- Involvement of the **palmar sesamoid bones**, more markedly the abaxial one, with moderate bone loss
- Less pronounced change at the proximal part of the **proximal phalanx of digit IV**
- Severe circumferential soft-tissue swelling centred on the **metacarpophalangeal joint IV**, with
  severe circumferential contrast enhancement, extending proximally to mid-diaphysis of MC IV and
  distally to the distal phalanx, both palmar-accentuated, and axially/abaxially into the
  interdigital spaces — following the course of the **deep digital flexor tendon** of that digit
- After contrast, a relatively hypoattenuating zone around the tendon (tendon sheath)
- **Severely enlarged left superficial cervical lymph node** (~1.8 cm), heterogeneous enhancement
- Mildly enlarged left accessory axillary lymph node

*CT diagnosis:* severe arthritis of the metacarpophalangeal joint IV with **polyostotic
osteomyelitis**, severe **tendovaginitis of the deep digital flexor tendon** (digit IV), and
severe accompanying soft-tissue swelling. Severe left superficial cervical lymphadenomegaly.

*CT conclusion:* findings suspicious for **septic arthritis and tenovaginitis with phlegmon and
reactive regional lymphadenomegaly** — e.g. following a bite or stepping on a sharp object.
Foreign material such as small plant fragments **not visible, though its presence cannot be
excluded.** Neoplastic causes (fibrosarcoma, squamous cell carcinoma) considered unlikely.

*Ultrasound:* severe circumferential dilatation of the tendon sheath around the terminal tendon of
extensor digitorum IV, with fibre pattern intact; punctate hyperechoic foci within one of the
flexors palmarolaterally at the level of the accessory carpal bone (most likely flexor carpi
ulnaris); small zones of corpuscular content along the palmar contour of the metacarpals; the
MCP joint of digit IV distended dorsally at the level of the punctate wound in the 3rd/4th
interdigital space, with mildly irregular joint margins.

*Ultrasound conclusion:* strong suspicion of a probably **septic tenovaginitis of the flexor
tendon sheath of MC IV** and **phlegmon** along the distal left forelimb, palmar-accentuated. The
hyperechoic foci remained unexplained — differentials small gas accumulations or fibrosis, **with
no clear indication of foreign material** (no peripheral halo, no associated muscle
heterogeneity). Fluid accumulations were too small to aspirate safely.

*Lymph node aspirate:* mild reactive hyperplasia — a non-specific finding secondary to antigenic
stimulation.

**Final diagnosis `[S2]`:** phlegmon of the left forelimb with signs of osteomyelitis of the left
distal extremity (left paw, digit IV). Cause unknown; a small foreign body suspected but never
demonstrated.

**Treatment & response `[S2]`:** inpatient IV antibiotics and analgesia. **Marked improvement in
gait within one day**, swelling almost fully resolved. **No surgical indication.** Discharged on
Clavaseptin 250 mg ½ tab TID for 4 weeks, Gabapentin 50 mg TID for 2 weeks, Buprenorphine 0.3 ml
2–3×/day for 1–2 days, with 1–2 weeks of rest.

**Outcome `[O]`:** the leg appeared normal afterwards. A recheck appointment was attended and the
Tierspital reported that everything was fine, but **no written report was received** and **no
follow-up bloodwork was performed**.
